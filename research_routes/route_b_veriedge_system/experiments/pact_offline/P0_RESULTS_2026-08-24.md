# PACT P0 result: full-coverage projection versus sparse adaptive attacks

Date: 2026-08-24  
Status: local geometry/protocol validation; not a paper-level FPR result

## Question

At the same full-vector L2 strength, does detection under PACT still collapse
when an attack shrinks from 256 modified coordinates to a single coordinate?

The coordinate-opening prototype had a hard support-hit ceiling: a sparse
attack could be invisible simply because none of its coordinates were opened.
PACT replaces coordinate opening with post-commit dense Rademacher projections,
so every tensor coordinate affects every projection row.

## Method

- Data: the real `stack_01` versus `stack_02` heterogeneous residuals in the
  local E2 live subset.
- Calibration: six prompts at each of C1, C2, and C3; 64 projection draws per
  prompt/checkpoint; nominal alpha 0.01.
- Evaluation: held-out prompts `eval_001` and `eval_005` at C1--C3.
- Attack supports: 1, 16, and 256 coordinates.
- Relative L2 strengths: rho = 0.01, 0.02, and 0.05.
- Projection rows: K = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, and 64.
- Adaptive selection: eight fixed candidate tensors per setting are compared on
  64 selection seeds. The least-detectable candidate is frozen separately for
  each public K policy and tested on 256 independent final seeds.
- Harm preservation: attacks with support at least 16 are orthogonal to the
  measured honest residual. A one-coordinate attack cannot be orthogonal in
  one dimension, so it is placed on a smallest-magnitude residual coordinate
  with a nonnegative cross term.

Calibration, selection, and final testing use disjoint deterministic seed
domains. All selected attacks at a boundary face the same final projection
matrices, giving paired comparisons without revealing those matrices during
candidate selection.

## Main result

PACT removes the support-hit ceiling, but support invariance is not exact at
very small K. With one projection row, a one-coordinate perturbation contributes
nearly fixed projected energy, whereas a denser signed perturbation has a
higher-variance, chi-square-like energy estimate. The resulting shape effect
shrinks as K averages more rows.

Mean adaptive detection rates across the six held-out prompt/checkpoints are:

| K | rho | support 1 | support 16 | support 256 | max--min |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | .02 | .652 | .404 | .392 | .260 |
| 1 | .05 | .913 | .704 | .701 | .212 |
| 8 | .02 | .887 | .886 | .875 | .012 |
| 8 | .05 | 1.000 | .995 | .995 | .005 |
| 16 | .01 | .764 | .738 | .723 | .041 |
| 16 | .02 | .926 | .915 | .911 | .015 |
| 16 | .05 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | .000 |
| 32 | .01 | .859 | .855 | .855 | .004 |
| 32 | .02 | .995 | .987 | .986 | .009 |
| 64 | .01 | .885 | .885 | .889 | .003 |
| 64 | .02 | 1.000 | .997 | 1.000 | .003 |

Thus K >= 32 is the clean local design point: the largest support-dependent
spread is below 0.9 percentage points for every tested strength. K = 16 is
already close at rho >= .02, but leaves a 4.1-point spread at rho = .01.

This is materially different from coordinate sampling. No curve is capped by
`q/N`, and increasing K improves concentration rather than merely increasing
the chance of touching the attack support.

## Constraint audit

- Maximum relative-L2 construction error: `6.94e-18`.
- Maximum absolute residual cosine for support 16 or 256: `4.52e-18`.
- One-coordinate candidates all have nonnegative residual cross terms.
- Reported rows: 378 = 6 boundaries x 7 K values x 3 strengths x 3 supports.
- Selection and final-test seed domains are distinct.

## Important calibration failure

The geometry result is positive; the current calibration result is not ready
for a claim. Mean held-out honest rates rise from 2.3% at K = 1 to 13.8% at
K = 64. This is almost entirely C3: at K = 64, C1 and C2 are about 1%, while C3
is 39% on the two held-out prompts.

Repeated projection seeds do not create independent calibration prompts. The
six-prompt threshold therefore underestimates cross-prompt variation at C3,
especially as larger K removes projection noise and exposes the mismatch in
the underlying full residual-energy distribution.

The correct interpretation is:

1. PACT's full-coverage statistic passes the sparse-support geometry test.
2. A fixed pooled empirical quantile from six prompts fails the deployment
   calibration test at C3.
3. The next design problem is boundary-conditional uncertainty and explicit
   `INCONCLUSIVE/escalate`, not more projection rows alone.

## Next experiments required for a strong Route B

1. Expand calibration to the full E2-R prompt corpus and use prompt-level,
   not seed-level, uncertainty estimates.
2. Compare fixed thresholds with conformal/upper-confidence calibration and an
   explicit abstention band.
3. Evaluate adaptive expansion K = 8 -> 16 -> 32 -> 64 and report evidence
   cost, false positive, detection, and inconclusive rate jointly.
4. Add semantic-harm attacks from the model pipeline; the present synthetic
   perturbations validate check geometry, not output harm.
5. Measure projection and commitment overhead on the three-node runtime.
